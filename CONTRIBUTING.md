# Contributing to CharityGraph Playbooks

**Status:** Contribution guidance, draft v0.1

Useful contributors include analysts and consultants, charity practitioners and advisers, funders, service planners, researchers and sector specialists. A contributor does not need to understand JSON Schema, YAML, Git or programming to make a useful contribution.

## Contribution ladder

1. Suggest an analytical question or idea.
2. Submit an existing prompt or method.
3. Develop a structured candidate Playbook.
4. Participate in evaluation and review.
5. Publish a Community Playbook or propose governed adoption/adaptation as an Official CharityGraph Playbook.

GitHub is a governance/publication backend for this repository, not necessarily the eventual public contribution interface. Low-friction forms or assisted submission may be added later.

## Official and Community status

An **Official CharityGraph Playbook** is reviewed and adopted under CharityGraph governance and maintained/released as an official CharityGraph method.

A **Community Playbook** is an identifiable, attributed contribution available in the ecosystem but not represented as CharityGraph's adopted analytical method. Popularity, ratings and voting do not confer official status.

Contribution records should preserve author attribution, adaptation lineage and relevant affiliation or conflict disclosure. Adoption of a community method does not erase its contribution history.

## Feedback classes

Keep feedback distinct:

1. a CharityGraph Data error follows the Data correction pathway;
2. a Playbook method/design problem follows Playbook correction and evaluation; and
3. an execution, model or retrieval problem follows execution/model feedback.

Do not submit private strategic parameters as part of a Playbook definition. Where practical, invocation generation should keep such parameters client-side and outside canonical CharityGraph Data.
